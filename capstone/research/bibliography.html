Annotated Bibliography

Harris, Tristan. “How Technology Hijacks People’s Minds.” Medium, 18 May 2016.

Visit Original Site

In this article, former Google design ethicist Tristan Harris discusses how technology platforms intentionally use psychological principles and persuasive design techniques to maximize user engagement and screen time. The article explains how notifications, recommendation systems, infinite scrolling, and social validation loops are carefully designed to capture and retain attention.

This source is useful for my project because it directly connects interface design to attention extraction and behavioral influence. It supports the mental model I want to challenge in my project: the belief that users are fully in control of their online behavior and attention.

The article helped me think more critically about how interaction design can shape emotions, habits, and decision making. It also inspired ideas for speculative visualizations showing attention as a measurable resource that companies actively compete to control.

Lewis, Paul. “'Our Minds Can Be Hijacked': The Tech Insiders Who Fear a Smartphone Dystopia.” The Guardian, 6 Oct. 2017.

Visit Original Site

This article explores concerns from former Silicon Valley employees and technology insiders about the addictive design strategies used by social media platforms and smartphone applications. It discusses how platforms are engineered to maximize engagement through psychological triggers such as notifications, rewards, and endless content feeds.

The article is useful for my project because it frames attention as an economic resource that companies aggressively compete for. It also supports the dystopian themes of my project by presenting real-world concerns about technology’s influence on behavior, mental health, and autonomy.

One important conclusion I reached from this source is that many designers and engineers within the technology industry are themselves concerned about the long-term consequences of persuasive technology systems. This reinforces the idea that the future of digital attention may become increasingly difficult for users to control.

“Attention Economy.” Wikipedia.

Visit Original Site

This article provides an overview of the concept of the attention economy, which describes human attention as a scarce and valuable resource that companies compete to capture and monetize. The article discusses the history of the term, its relationship to advertising systems, and its connection to digital media platforms and internet culture.

This source is useful because it provides foundational definitions and historical context for many of the ideas explored in my project, including attention extraction, advertising systems, and behavioral influence. It also helped me better understand how attention became integrated into modern digital business models.

My main conclusion from this source is that attention has increasingly become a measurable economic commodity in online environments. This idea directly supports the central speculative concept of my project, which imagines a future where attention is openly treated as a market resource.
